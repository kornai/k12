\section{Introduction}
Guinean Sign Language (ISO 639-3 gus) is the sign language of the deaf community of Guinea (Conakry). Searches turned up almost no documentation of the language itself. Much of the material found concerns Guinea-Bissau, a different country with a different sign language, and is not used here.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
A 2019 Gu\'een\'ews report describes the deaf schools of Conakry (e.g.\ Boulbinet school in Kaloum, under the Ministry of Social Action) and criticism of their curricula by the disability network ROPACIDPH: \url{https://guineenews.org/2019/02/26/education-le-ropacidph-demande-un-transfert-de-tutelle-pour-les-ecoles-des-sourds-de-conakry/}. No sign-language textbooks were found.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
A Leiden University sign-language map lists Guinean Sign Language in Conakry: \url{https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3593897/download}. A study in \emph{Health Sciences and Disease} on childhood deafness among pupils of a Conakry deaf school: \url{https://hsd-fmsb.org/index.php/hsd/article/download/807/pdf_430/2396}. No dictionary or grammar was found.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
Gallaudet University lists deaf organizations in Guinea, including the Association Guin\'eenne des Sourds: \url{https://gallaudet.edu/academic-affairs/international-relations/deaf-organizations-in-guinea/}. Joshua Project profile of the Deaf in Guinea (faith-based source): \url{https://www.joshuaproject.net/index.php/people_groups/19007/GV}.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
No video or digital resources found.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Deaf education in Conakry exists but appears under-resourced and without a documented sign-language curriculum. Work would begin with the Association Guin\'eenne des Sourds and the deaf schools, with documentation of the language and training of deaf teachers.
