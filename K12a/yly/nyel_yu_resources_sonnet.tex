\section{Introduction}
Ny\^el\^ayu (also written Ny\^alayu or Y\^alayu; ISO 639-3 yly; Glottocode nyal1254) is a Kanak (Austronesian) language of northern New Caledonia, with about 2,000 speakers. Search results describe two dialects that are not mutually intelligible. Belep (Pooc or Haat) is spoken on the Belep islands, and Puma (Paak or Ovac) is spoken around Poum, Pouébo and Balade. Sources disagree on its endangerment status.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
My search found no primers, textbooks or school programmes for Ny\^el\^ayu. It also did not turn up a dedicated dictionary.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item McCracken, Chelsea Leigh (2012), \emph{A grammar of Belep}, a ProQuest thesis. The Endangered Languages Project lists it, and I saw only the catalogue entry. \url{https://endangeredlanguages.com/lang/10924}
\item The ABVD has a Belep-dialect wordlist with French glosses. \url{https://abvd.eva.mpg.de/austronesian/language.php?id=1480}
\item The English Wikipedia article gives an overview. \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ny%C3%A2layu_language}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
I found no scripture translations or community organisations in my searches.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
Omniglot has pages on the language and its number words, which draw on McCracken's grammar. I found no audio or video. \url{https://omniglot.com/writing/nyelayu.htm} \url{https://omniglot.com/language/numbers/nyelayu.htm}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Existing material is mostly academic: one grammar of the Belep dialect and a wordlist. A curriculum would need to be built from these with local speakers, and would probably also need New Caledonian (French-language) educational sources that I did not find.
