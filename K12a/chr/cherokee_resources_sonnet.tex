\section{Introduction}
Cherokee (ISO 639-3 chr) is an endangered Iroquoian language of the United States, written in the Sequoyah syllabary. The Cherokee Nation (Western/Overhill dialect) and the Eastern Band (Eastern/Kituwah dialect) have different varieties, so materials from one may not fit the other. This report lists only resources that appeared in web search results.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Cherokee Immersion School (Cherokee Nation). Instruction follows Oklahoma grade-level standards but is delivered exclusively in Cherokee, with the syllabary used for all print materials. \url{https://language.cherokee.gov/cherokee-immersion-school/}
\item Cherokee Nation Language Department, free teaching materials: printable syllabary flashcards, stencils, a quiz, a chart and a handwriting guide, plus the \emph{See, Say, Write} textbook with MP3 audio and \emph{We Are Learning Cherokee}. \url{https://language.cherokee.gov/learning-materials/teaching-materials/}
\end{itemize}

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Cherokee Nation Dictionary app, a searchable digitization of the Durbin Feeling Cherokee-English Dictionary with audio, example sentences, grammar notes and an interactive syllabary chart. \url{https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6748102942}
\item Cherokee Nation language revitalization project report (2005), describing curriculum development work such as translated children's books and games. \url{https://www.nnigovernance.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/2005_HN_Cherokee_language_revitalization_project.pdf}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
The Language Department notes that Cherokee speakers have used the Cherokee New Testament to teach themselves to read the syllabary (see the teaching materials page above). I did not find a separate stable scripture URL in the searches.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
A news item reports that the School of the Seven Directions, a Cherokee-language grades 4--8 school beside the Durbin Feeling Language Center in Tahlequah, is now operated independently by the Nation. \url{https://nativeamericanstoday.com/?p=526} This report is a starting list, not a catalog of current curriculum products; the Language Department should be contacted for details.
