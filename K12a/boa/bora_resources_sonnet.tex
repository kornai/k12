\section{Introduction}
Bora (ISO 639-3 boa; Glottocode bora1263) is a severely endangered Boran language of the Peru--Colombia border region (Loreto in Peru; Amazonas in Colombia), also called Mira\~na in the closely related Colombian variety. Sources give roughly 2,000--3,000 speakers in total, about 1,000 of them in northern Peru. Adults are generally speakers, but children now learn Spanish and the language is not being transmitted to them. Web search found no evidence of a formal bilingual education programme for Bora in either country, and few materials beyond linguistic descriptions. This report lists only what was found in search results.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No primers, textbooks or school programmes for Bora turned up in search. The Peruvian Ministry of Education is reported to be standardizing the Bora alphabet with Bora participation (see the Endangered Languages Project entry):
\begin{itemize}
\item Endangered Languages Project, Bora entry: \url{https://endangeredlanguages.com/lang/1854}
\item A 2020 Universidad Pedag\'ogica Nacional thesis describes a pedagogical proposal developed with Bora-Mira\~na communities in Amazonas, Colombia (an approach built on local educational practices and an ecological calendar, not a documented bilingual programme): \url{https://repositorio.upn.edu.co/bitstreams/5d96ad2a-034b-4cd8-bc4b-a7e4dc1f0935/download}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
Search results note that the written form was developed by Wycliffe/SIL missionaries, but no specific scripture or community-organisation pages were found.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
No audio, video, corpus or software resources were found in search results. The Endangered Languages Project entry for Bora (\url{https://endangeredlanguages.com/node/25833}) is a status record only.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Bora has no known school presence and weak intergenerational transmission, so a K-12 programme would be a revitalization effort starting from reference grammars (above) and community work with remaining adult speakers. Availability of the SIL dictionary and any texts should be confirmed with SIL, the Peruvian Ministry of Education and Bora communities directly.
