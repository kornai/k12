\section{Introduction}
Montenegrin (ISO 639-3 cnr) is the official language of Montenegro. This report rests on one round of searches and lists only items seen in results; no school textbooks or ministry curriculum documents were found.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
None found for K-12. The following textbook-type works are aimed at learners and cover the Bosnian/Croatian/Montenegrin/Serbian group:
\begin{itemize}
\item \v Zeljko Vrabec, \emph{Bosnian, Croatian, Montenegrin and Serbian: An Essential Grammar} (Routledge, 2022): \url{https://hoepli.it/amp/bosnian-croatian-montenegrin-and-serbian/9780367723637.html}
\end{itemize}

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item University of Oregon library guide to Montenegrin, listing the 2016 \emph{Dictionary of Montenegrin National and Literary Language} (Montenegrin Academy of Sciences and Arts): \url{https://researchguides.uoregon.edu/slavic-languages/montenegrin}
\item Sonja Nenezi\'c, ``Grammatical Data in the Dictionary of Montenegrin National and Literary Language'', \emph{Lexikos}: \url{https://lexikos.journals.ac.za/pub/article/view/1908}
\item Lexilogos Montenegrin dictionary links: \url{https://www.lexilogos.com/montenegrin_dictionnaire.htm}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
Nothing relevant was found in the search results.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
Only the Lexilogos link above (online dictionary pointers) was found.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Montenegrin was standardized recently, and the dictionary article notes the 2016 dictionary is the only one of its kind. Native-language school materials (Ministry of Education, Institute for Montenegrin Language and Literature) were not located in these searches and should be sought directly.
