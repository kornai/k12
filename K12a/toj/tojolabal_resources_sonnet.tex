\section{Introduction}
This dossier covers Tojolabal (ISO 639-3 toj; Glottocode tojo1241), a Mayan language of Chiapas, Mexico, with about 34,000 speakers. A single search was run. It found official school materials from Mexico's SEP but no dictionary or grammar.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item A Tojolabal literacy textbook from Chiapas, produced by the Direcci\'on General de Educaci\'on Ind\'igena (SEP), author Concepci\'on de Jes\'us G\'omez L\'opez: \url{https://dgeiib.basica.sep.gob.mx/files/fondo-editorial/acervo-historico/ah_00081.pdf}
\item Tojolabal literature titles (2018) in the Biblioteca Escolar Ind\'igena y Migrante series, including \emph{Compa\~nero tojol-ab'al}, with audiobook CDs. These are SEP resource pages (the specific resource number for each title was not confirmed): \url{https://nuevaescuelamexicana.sep.gob.mx/contenido/recurso/41529/} and \url{https://nuevaescuelamexicana.sep.gob.mx/contenido/recurso/41547/}
\end{itemize}

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
No dictionary or grammar was found in the search. Sources such as INALI and academic linguistic databases should be searched separately.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
None found in search results.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
The 2018 literature books are reported to come with audiobook CDs. Other SEP resource pages in the same series were returned: \url{https://nuevaescuelamexicana.sep.gob.mx/contenido/recurso/41464/}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The SEP produces Tojolabal literacy and literature materials within its indigenous education system, so the claim that the language is not known to be taught may be understated. Further search is needed for the full curriculum, teacher materials and a reference dictionary.
