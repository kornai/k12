\section{Introduction}
Mal\'eku Ja\'ika (also Malecu, Guatuso; ISO 639-3 gut) is a Chibchan language of north-central Costa Rica, spoken in Margarita, Tonjibe and El Sol. It is endangered, with a shift to Spanish among younger people. Sources indicate an official Latin-script alphabet adopted by the Ministry of Public Education, based on the work of Adolfo Constenla Uma\~na, with some use in primary schools and as a subject in a secondary school (reported by Omniglot; confirm with the Ministry).

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
The University of Costa Rica project ``Diversidad ling\"u\'istica de Costa Rica'' produced the coloring-book readers \emph{Mu\'erra \'ofa p\'oto mar\'ama} in Malecu; two of the volumes are available: \url{https://dipalicori.ucr.ac.cr/wp-content/uploads/Libro-de-muerra_01.pdf} and \url{https://www.dipalicori.ucr.ac.cr/wp-content/uploads/Libro-de-muerra_02.pdf}.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
A Malecu--Spanish / Spanish--Malecu dictionary by Adolfo Constenla (completed by Carlos S\'anchez) was published by Editorial Tecnol\'ogica de Costa Rica and is reported at \url{https://tec.ac.cr/hoyeneltec/print/4628}. Constenla's \emph{Gram\'atica de la lengua guatusa} is cited in the same search results but no direct link was seen. Overview: \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal\%C3\%A9ku_language}; alphabet and writing: \url{https://www.omniglot.com/writing/maleku.htm}.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
A starting base exists: an official alphabet, a dictionary, a grammar, and early readers. Missing are graded textbooks beyond early reading and teacher materials; the dictionary and the UCR readers are natural foundations, to be developed with the three communities.
