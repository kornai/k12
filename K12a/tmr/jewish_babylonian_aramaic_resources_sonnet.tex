\section{Introduction}
Jewish Babylonian Aramaic (ISO 639-3 tmr) is the language of the Babylonian Talmud and Geonic literature. It has no native speakers and no ethnic community. It is learned as a second language, mainly in religious study. The search found catalogue records for print reference works but few free online resources.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No school primers or K-12 programmes were found in the search.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Michael Sokoloff, \emph{A Dictionary of Jewish Babylonian Aramaic of the Talmudic and Geonic Periods} (Bar-Ilan University Press and Johns Hopkins University Press, 2002). This is the standard lexicon; the search found only bookseller and library records. The search did not confirm an online edition. \url{https://syri.ac/bibliography/789413724}
\item Bibliographic record for the same dictionary. \url{https://katalog.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/titel/65626176}
\item Jastrow's dictionary of the Targumim, Talmud and Midrash is described in a bookseller listing as a classic reference. The search did not show whether it is freely available online. \url{https://www.foxtalebookshoppe.com/book/9789562914741}
\item FU Berlin Judaistik list of online resources. The search did not show which items cover Aramaic. \url{https://www.geschkult.fu-berlin.de/en/e/judaistik/Linkliste/Ressources/index.html}
\end{itemize}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
A heritage or revitalization curriculum would need to be built from the Talmudic text and the printed lexicons and grammars. No ready-made K-12 materials were found. Further searching is needed for online lexicon tools, grammars and graded readers.
