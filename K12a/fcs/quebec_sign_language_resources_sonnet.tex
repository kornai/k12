\section{Introduction}
Quebec Sign Language (LSQ, ISO 639-3 fcs) is the sign language of the deaf community of Quebec. This report is based on a single web search; it found research and policy documents about LSQ in education but no ready-made K-12 teaching materials.
\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No textbooks, primers or ministry curriculum documents for LSQ were found in the search results. The following policy document bears directly on LSQ as a language of instruction.
\begin{itemize}
\item OPHQ report on LSQ and improved services for deaf signing persons (2013), arguing that learning LSQ and using it as a language of instruction supports school success: \url{https://www.ophq.gouv.qc.ca/fileadmin/centre_documentaire/Etudes__analyses_et_rapports/OPHQ_Rapport_LSQ.pdf}
\end{itemize}
\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Luna (2015), study of syntactic variation in LSQ among older signers: \url{https://cla-acl.artsci.utoronto.ca/pdfs/actes-2015/Luna-2015.pdf}
\item Article in \textit{Globe} (2013) on how service organisation hinders recognition of LSQ as a deaf child's first language of learning: \url{https://www.erudit.org/en/journals/globe/2013-v16-n1-globe0796/1018177ar.pdf}
\end{itemize}
\section{Religious and Community Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Montreal memory centre, history of the Montreal deaf community and its institutions: \url{https://ville.montreal.qc.ca/memoiresdesmontrealais/la-communaute-sourde-de-montreal-et-ses-institutions?page=8}
\end{itemize}
\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
No video, corpus or software resources were found in the search results.
\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The documents above describe the policy and historical context: specialised institutions taught in signs until about 1960, and integration into regular schools followed. A BAnQ document records a 2003 request to examine recognising LSQ as a language of instruction: \url{https://collections.banq.qc.ca/document/s/t/y7Sv2_eNFeswIiaNrRPzlA}. Current school resources would have to be sought from the Quebec education ministry and deaf community organisations; none were verified here.
