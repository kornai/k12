\section{Introduction}
Toromono (also Toromona; ISO 639-3 tno; Glottocode toro1255) is a Western Tacanan language of northwestern Bolivia. Sources describe it as poorly attested, known from wordlists, and severely endangered or possibly extinct. About 200 people were reported in 1983 and have not been located since. The Toromona are also described as an uncontacted people living near the upper Madidi and Heath Rivers. Bolivia's Administrative Resolution 48/2006 created a protected zone for them within Madidi National Park. Joshua Project gives a population of about 300 and says most speak Spanish. These sources conflict and are weakly sourced.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
None exist as far as the search shows. Given the reported uncontacted status, any curriculum work must respect the community's choice not to be contacted.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
There is no basis for a speaker-led revitalization curriculum. Any work would rely on the old wordlists and on better-documented Tacanan relatives (for example Tacana, Araona, Cavine\~na, Ese Ejja), which I did not search. Given the uncontacted status and the protected zone, no outreach should be planned.
