\section{Introduction}
Swiss-French Sign Language (Langue des Signes de Suisse romande, LSF-SR; ISO 639-3 ssr; Glottocode swis1241) is a deaf community sign language of French-speaking Switzerland. The Endangered Languages Project lists it as endangered, with about 1,700 native signers (an old figure traced to roughly 2009--2010). It lists regional varieties tied to deaf schools in Geneva, Lausanne, Neuchatel, Fribourg and Sion. LSF-SR is distinct from Swiss German Sign Language (DSGS), and it resembles French Sign Language (LSF). The searches found very little on K-12 materials. This report is therefore short.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
I found no LSF-SR textbook, primer or curriculum in search results. A 2015 profile on the Endangered Languages Project page says the language is taught in deaf schools and in bilingual (sign and French) education in Geneva, Fribourg and Lausanne. I could not verify this. See \url{https://endangeredlanguages.com/lang/7360/bibliography?hl=es}.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
A Scripture Earth page for code ssr appeared in the results. I did not check whether it holds any sign-language scripture content: \url{https://www.scriptureearth.org/00eng.php?iso=ssr}. I found no community organisation pages for LSF-SR.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
The speech2sign project at the University of Geneva (\url{https://speech2sign.unige.ch/index.php/download_file/view/16/134}) is linked to the Trainslate application. I could not verify whether it offers LSF-SR video content. I found no video dictionary or corpus in the results.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Little openly documented material was found. A curriculum would need to be built with Deaf signers of Romandy, the deaf schools, and university sign language researchers. The existing sources are old, and their figures should be treated as rough. A dictionary is mentioned in the 2015 profile, but no specific one was identified.
