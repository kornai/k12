\section{Introduction}
Fania (ISO 639-3 \texttt{fni}; Glottocode fani1244), also called Kulaale (speakers call themselves Kulaawe), is a Bua-group Adamawa language of the Guéra region of Chad, spoken in about a dozen villages. Estimates of speakers run from about 1,000 to 1,100 (Global Recordings Network gives a population of 2,900). It is endangered: in towns such as Sarh and N'Djamena it is usually not passed on to children. Northern and southern dialects differ considerably.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Fania has no known written standard or school use, and few documented materials. A curriculum would first need an agreed orthography and community-led materials; the Lionnet snapshot is the best starting point for the linguistic facts.
