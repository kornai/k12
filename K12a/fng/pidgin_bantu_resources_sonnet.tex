\section{Introduction}
Pidgin Bantu (Fanagalo, Fanakalo; ISO 639-3 \texttt{fng}) is a Zulu-based pidgin of southern Africa with no ethnic community of its own and no known use as a medium of school instruction. The web search found only a small number of bibliographic and reference items; full texts were not seen, so their contents are not described beyond what the listings state.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No school textbooks or primers for classroom use were found. The closest items are older learner-oriented works: J.\ D.\ Bold's \emph{Fanagalo phrase book, grammar and dictionary} (first edition 1951, many later editions), listed by a used bookseller: \url{https://quaggabooks.co.za/product/dictionary-grammar-and-phrase-book-of-fanagalo-bold-j-d-2/}. Opperman's 1967 \emph{Fanakalo Textbook} is cited in the Atlas of Pidgin and Creole Language Structures (see below), but no copy or link was found.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Fanagalo is a second-language lingua franca (historically in mining and industry) with substantial Zulu vocabulary. A native-language K-12 curriculum is not applicable in the usual sense; any educational material would most plausibly build on Zulu-language curricula. The resources above are reference and historical rather than pedagogical.
