\section{Introduction}
Djabwurrung (Djab Wurrung; ISO 639-3 tjw) is an Aboriginal language of western Victoria, around Stawell, Ararat and the Grampians. It has no first-language speakers now. Sources disagree on whether to call it dormant or extinct. Some of the material found is for the related Djadja Wurrung, so it may not apply to Djabwurrung.
\section{Primary Educational Resources}
None found specifically for Djabwurrung.
\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item VACL (Victorian Aboriginal Corporation for Languages), Djab Wurrung language page, with historical spellings, language area and placename references. \url{https://vacl.org.au/language/djab-wurrung/}
\item Victorian Collections, search for ``wurrung'' (185 items). \url{https://victoriancollections.net.au/collections?q=wurrung}
\item Wikipedia, Djabwurrung language. \url{https://wikipedia.com/wiki/ISO_639:tjw}
\item State Library Victoria guide, ``Finding the meaning of an Aboriginal word''. \url{https://guides.slv.vic.gov.au/aboriginalwords/steps}
\end{itemize}
The VACL catalogue also lists Tully (1997) on Djadja Wurrung, a dictionary with grammar notes, and Sholl (1996) word lists. I did not see direct URLs for them.
\section{Religious and Community Resources}
VACL is the community language organisation named in the results. A forum thread mentioned a Dja Wurrung community Facebook page, but it is informal and I give no link.
\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
None found.
\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Material is limited to historical word lists and VACL records. Any curriculum should be developed with VACL and the relevant traditional owner groups, and the Djabwurrung and Djadja Wurrung sources should be kept apart.
