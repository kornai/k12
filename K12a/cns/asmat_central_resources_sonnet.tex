\section{Introduction}
Central Asmat (ISO 639-3 cns) is an endangered Trans-New Guinea language of Papua, Indonesia. Search results show little school-oriented material. The best-documented variety is Kawenak, and several sources describe older work on related varieties. Sources should be checked for which variety they cover.
\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No primer or textbook for Central Asmat turned up in the searches. A school curriculum would have to be built from the dictionary and scripture materials below.
\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Calvin Roesler's unpublished Asmat Dictionary (Asmat-Indonesian-English), archived at PARADISEC; the archive notes the Indonesian glosses need checking by a bilingual speaker: \url{https://catalog.paradisec.org.au/repository/CR1/dictionary}
\item Peter Drabbe (1959), Dictionary of the Asmat Language, listed in Glottolog: \url{https://glottolog.org/resource/reference/id/475473}
\item C. L. Voorhoeve (1980) Asmat-related reference, listed in Glottolog: \url{https://glottolog.org/resource/reference/id/85998}
\item New Guinea World overview page for Central Asmat: \url{https://newguineaworld.linguistik.uzh.ch/families/trans-new-guinea/asmat-muli-strait/asmat-kamrau-bay/asmat-kamoro/asmat/central-asmat}

\end{itemize}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The language is not known to be taught in schools. Documentation is mostly older, mission-linked and variety-specific. Any curriculum needs local community involvement, speaker review of the dictionary data, and new primers.
