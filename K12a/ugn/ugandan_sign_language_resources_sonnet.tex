\section{Introduction}
Ugandan Sign Language (USL, ISO 639-3 ugn) is the sign language of Uganda's deaf community. This report is based on a small number of web searches. Little openly available curriculum material was found, and the entries below are thin and mostly secondary.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No official USL-medium K-12 curriculum or textbook series was found. A 2024 Kyambogo University master's thesis (Betty Isoto) studies secondary teachers' views on USL as an elective under the competency-based lower secondary curriculum. It recommends more qualified sign language teachers and more instructional materials. Record: \url{https://kyuspace.kyu.ac.ug/items/b00791e8-f131-46b7-9233-4dd4ea40b24e}

A KFM news report says advocates are urging the government to develop a sign language syllabus. This is advocacy, not an adopted curriculum: \url{https://www.kfm.co.ug/govt-urged-to-develop-sign-language-syllabus}

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
The \emph{Uganda Sign Language Dictionary} (eds. Lars Wallin, Dorothy Lule, Sam Lutalo, Bonny Busingye; Kampala, Kyambogo University Sign Language Dictionary Research Project, 2006, about 444 pp.) appears in a library catalogue: \url{https://catalogue.umu.ac.ug/bib/38718}

General background: \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugandan_Sign_Language}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
None found in the searches.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
No online video or digital corpus was found in the searches.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
USL has no verified adopted K-12 curriculum in the sources found. Kyambogo University and the Ministry of Education are the natural contacts for follow-up. The dictionary's availability and any newer edition should be confirmed.
