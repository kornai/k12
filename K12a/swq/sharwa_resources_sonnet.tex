\section{Introduction}
Sharwa (ISO 639-3: swq; Glottocode shar1249) is a Central Chadic (Afro-Asiatic) language of northern Cameroon. Alternative names are Tchevi, Sherwin and Sarwaye. About 5,100 speakers were counted in 2000. Sources note a shift towards Fulfulde. Wikipedia describes it as closely related to Jimi.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
The searches found no primers, textbooks or school materials for Sharwa.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}

\item Abstract by Richard Gravina (WOCAL) comparing Sharwa and Jimi (vowels, consonants, prosody): \url{https://www.uni-koeln.de/phil-fak/afrikanistik/wocal/schedule/abstracts/4-6-2%20Richard%20Gravina.pdf}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Very little exists. No orthography or school materials were found. The survey and the Sharwa--Jimi phonology work are the starting points for developing a writing system. Fulfulde pressure suggests the community would need to be consulted on any school use.
