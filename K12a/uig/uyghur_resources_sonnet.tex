\section{Introduction}
Uyghur (ISO 639-3 uig) is a Turkic language with about 10 million speakers, written mainly in the Arabic script. The searches found mostly university-level learner materials and technical resources. No official K-12 curricula, school textbooks or ministry materials were found in the results, so this dossier is limited.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
Georgetown University Press, \emph{Uyghur: An Elementary Textbook} (Nazarova and Niyaz), with multimedia: \url{https://press.georgetown.edu/Book/Uyghur}. University of Texas Press, \emph{Uyghur: An Intermediate Textbook}: \url{https://utpdistribution.com/9781626163645/uyghur}. These are adult and university learner texts in English, not school curricula.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
The University of Kansas Uyghur Language Guide lists textbooks, dictionaries, grammar, readers, audio and video: \url{https://guides.lib.ku.edu/uyghur}. Lexilogos lists Uyghur dictionaries (including Schwarz 1992 and St. John 1999 Uyghur-English) and a keyboard tool: \url{https://www.lexilogos.com/english/uyghur_dictionary.htm}.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
None were found in the searches.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
The UKIJ project reports an Uyghur input method and Unicode fonts: \url{https://ukij.org/en/index.php/projects/}. A Freedesktop.org bug report records a Uyghur keyboard layout based on GB 21669-2008: \url{https://bugs.freedesktop.org/show_bug.cgi?id=32576}.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Uyghur is described as a language of instruction, but no curriculum documents were located. A native-language curriculum would need materials sourced directly from the relevant education authorities and community bodies, which these searches did not reach.
