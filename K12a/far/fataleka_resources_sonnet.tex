\section{Introduction}
Fataleka (ISO 639-3 \texttt{far}; Glottocode fata1245) is an Austronesian language of Malaita, Solomon Islands, with roughly 6,700 speakers according to the survey brief. It is not known to be taught in schools. Web searches found very little: essentially a New Testament and a community profile. This report lists only what appeared in search results.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No primer, textbook or school programme for Fataleka was found.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
No grammar, orthography description or standalone dictionary was found. The search summary indicated that the eBible edition may include a ``Diksonari'' section, but its contents and size were not verified.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Farongoe Lea Ana Fataleka}, the Fataleka New Testament (translation credited to SITAG, Wycliffe Bible Translators; CC BY-ND 4.0), PDF: \url{https://eBible.org/pdf/far/far_book.pdf}
\item Same New Testament, ePub: \url{https://www.ebible.org/epub/far.epub}
\item Joshua Project people-group profile (population figure there, about 14,000, differs from the brief's speaker count): \url{https://joshuaproject.net/people_groups/11192}
\end{itemize}

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
No audio, video or software resources were found.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Material in Fataleka is scarce. A curriculum would need to build on the New Testament text as a written-language base and on new community-produced materials, in collaboration with Fataleka speakers. English and Solomon Islands Pijin resources were not searched here.
