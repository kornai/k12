\section{Introduction}
Seki (also Sekiyani, Sekiana, Baseke or Sheke; ISO 639-3 syi, Glottocode seki1238) is a Bantu language of Gabon and Equatorial Guinea, in Guthrie group B.21. Estimates of its speakers differ between sources (12,000 to about 15,000). The search found no dedicated dictionary, grammar or school material.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
None found. Seki is not known to be taught in schools.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
Omniglot's sample text is a Hail Mary. Joshua Project has a page on Seki in Gabon. \url{https://www.joshuaproject.net/index.php/people_groups/19171/GB}

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
Wikitongues has a page for the language code, which lists the language as unwritten. \url{https://wikitongues.org/languages/syi/} No audio corpus was found in the search.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Little exists. A curriculum would first need community-led documentation: a standard orthography, a lexicon and recordings. Sources disagree on whether the language is written, and Omniglot says Latin script. The French-medium schooling context in Gabon and the Spanish-medium context in Equatorial Guinea matter for planning, but this was not researched here.
