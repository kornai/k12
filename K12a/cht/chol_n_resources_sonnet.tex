\section{Introduction}
Cholón (ISO 639-3 cht) is an extinct language of the Huallaga valley, northern Peru, related to the poorly attested Híbito. No native speakers or community programmes were found; the sources are historical and academic.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
None found. No school materials, primers or programmes for Cholón appeared in the searches.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Pedro de la Mata, \emph{Arte de la Lengua Cholona} (1748), the principal source, and the 1772 Clota transcription in the British Library, discussed in Alexander-Bakkerus's \emph{Eighteenth-Century Cholón} (Leiden dissertation, 2005). \url{https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/en/research/research-output/humanities/eighteenth-century-cholon}
\item Full text of the Leiden dissertation. \url{https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A2887508/view}
\item ``Híbito and Cholón'', chapter in \emph{The Oxford Guide to the Languages of the Central Andes}. \url{https://academic.oup.com/book/59575/chapter/503209455}
\item SAPhon phonological inventory of Cholón. \url{https://linguistics.berkeley.edu/saphon/en/inv/Cholon.html}
\item Híbito language (Wikipedia), for the related language. \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%ADbito_language}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
None found beyond the missionary grammar above.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
No audio exists for an extinct language; only the phonological inventory above was found.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
A heritage curriculum would have to draw on the historical grammar and scholarly reconstruction, framed as cultural and linguistic heritage of the region rather than language instruction.
