\section{Introduction}
Hausa Sign Language (HSL; ISO 639-3 hsl) is a deaf community sign language of northern Nigeria. Very little formal educational material exists. This report lists only what turned up in web searches; no complete K-12 curriculum in HSL was found.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Thematic Hausa Sign Language dictionary booklets from the Kano-based hausa-sign-language.com project (worked with deaf people in Kano State since 2011; nine books reported). They pair sign drawings with Hausa glosses, are described as usable for teaching deaf people to read and write Hausa, and are distributed free to schools for deaf children, adult literacy classes and deaf associations. \url{https://hausa-sign-language.com/?p=9037}
\item Publisher listing for the series \emph{Maganar hannu / Hausa Sign Language} (booklets with sign drawings, Hausa glosses, English translations in an appendix). \url{https://buske.de/hbv_en/series/Maga}
\end{itemize}

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Wikitongues profile of Sani Muhammad Jimada, who is building an HSL dictionary with over 1,000 documented signs, planned with video in digital and print formats. \url{https://wikitongues.org/fellows/sani-muhammad-jimada/}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Joshua Project entry ``Deaf in Nigeria'' (community and missionary-oriented background). \url{https://www.joshuaproject.net/index.php/people_groups/19007/NI}
\end{itemize}

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
No standalone audio resources were found; the planned digital video dictionary above is the only digital item identified, and its release status was not verified.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
\begin{itemize}
\item Article on a foundation urging integration of sign language into the curriculum for persons with disabilities in Nigeria. \url{https://leadership.ng/foundation-pushes-for-integration-of-sign-language-in-curriculum-for-persons-with-disabilities/}
\item Paper on bilingual sign language and language development of learners with hearing impairment in Nigeria; it reports a lack of formal recognition of Nigerian Sign Language, limited teacher training and scarce learning resources. \url{https://cnlse.es/es/recursos/biblioteca/imperatives-of-bilingual-sign-language-in-language-development-of-learners-with-hearing-impairment-in-nigeria}
\end{itemize}
Nigerian Sign Language is the main language of deaf education; HSL-medium schooling appears not to exist. A curriculum would have to be built largely from scratch.
