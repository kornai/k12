\section{Introduction}
Eyak (ISO 639-3 \texttt{eya}) is a dormant language of south-central Alaska; its last native speaker died in 2008. Documentation was led by Michael Krauss. This report lists only what a web search turned up, and is oriented to heritage and revitalization.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No classroom materials or school programmes were found in the search results.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Alaska Native Language Archive, Guide to the Eyak Language Collection: \url{https://uaf.edu/anla/collections/eyak/index.php}
\item Overview of the Eyak Language (ANLA): \url{https://uaf.edu/anla/collections/eyak/about/index.php}
\item Omniglot page on Eyak: \url{https://omniglot.com/writing/eyak.php}
\item Michael E. Krauss (Wikipedia), background on the principal documenter: \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_E._Krauss}
\end{itemize}
The archive describes retranscription of grammar, dictionary and texts; whether a final published dictionary is available online was not confirmed.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
None identified in the search results.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
The ANLA collection is the main source of recordings and manuscripts; consult it directly for what is digitised.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
With no first-language speakers, a curriculum would rest on archival documentation. Any use should be coordinated with Eyak community members and the archive.
