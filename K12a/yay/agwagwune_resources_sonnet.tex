\section{Introduction}
Agwagwune (ISO 639-3: yay; Glottocode agwa1247), also called Gwune, is an Upper Cross River language of Cross River State, Nigeria. Wikipedia describes it as a dialect cluster with about 20,000 speakers (1973 figure). It lists Abayongo, Abini, Dim (Adim), Orum, Erei and Etono among the varieties. Joshua Project places the community in Biase Local Government Area. Searches found no textbooks, primers or school materials.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No primers, textbooks or school programmes in Agwagwune were found. The reference description says the language is not known to be taught in schools.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The language is stable and used by the whole community, but nothing educational was found. Any curriculum would require new materials, an agreed orthography and a choice of dialect (Abayongo, Erei and others). The Global Recordings audio and the numeral list are the only language data found. Community and local government contacts would be needed, and none were found in these searches.
