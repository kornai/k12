\section{Introduction}
Katcha-Kadugli-Miri (ISO 639-3 xtc; Glottocode katc1249) belongs to the Kadu (Kadugli-Krongo) languages and is spoken in South Kordofan, Sudan. Wikipedia gives about 75,000 native speakers (2004), while Joshua Project, citing Ethnologue 2016, gives 3,100. These figures conflict and should be checked. Katcha, Kadugli proper and Miri are the three most divergent varieties, and scholars disagree on whether they are dialects or separate languages. The search results contained no school materials.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No primers, textbooks or school programmes were found. Global Recordings Network states that the varieties share one orthography and the same literacy materials (per Ethnologue), but no such materials were located.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Documented resources are scarce and mostly religious. Arabic is widely spoken alongside the language. A curriculum would need new community-based development, and the dialect question (Katcha, Kadugli, Miri) would have to be settled first.
