\section{Introduction}
Nubaca (ISO 639-3 \texttt{baf}; also called Baca, Bango or Bongo) is a language of central Cameroon, spoken in the Bokito subdivision. Speaker estimates vary widely (roughly 4,500 to 8,400 in the sources found). One source rates it as threatened and unwritten. Very little material exists, and no school materials were found.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No primers, textbooks or school programmes in Nubaca were found in search results. Joshua Project reports no active literacy programme among the Bango/Nubaca: \url{https://www.joshuaproject.net/people_groups/10652/CM}.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
Joshua Project states that Scripture exists in French and Ewondo but not in Nubaca: \url{https://joshuaproject.net/people_groups/10652}. Language page: \url{https://joshuaproject.net/index.php/languages/baf}.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
Global Recordings Network lists a Nubaca page (names and varieties): \url{https://globalrecordings.net/en/language/baf}. Wikitongues language page: \url{https://wikitongues.org/languages/baf/}. I did not verify what audio content, if any, these pages hold.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The language appears to be unwritten with no known orthography or teaching materials. A curriculum would first need community-led orthography development and documentation; the Sebineni thesis on noun classes is the only substantial linguistic description located. French and Ewondo are the likely bridging languages.
