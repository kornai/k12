\section{Introduction}
Enggano (ISO 639-3 eno; Glottocode engg1245) is an Austronesian language of Enggano Island off Sumatra, Indonesia. A 2024 paper gives about 1,500 speakers and notes shift toward Indonesian and no formal educational use. An Oxford-led documentation project has produced most of the modern resources.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
The Oxford project aimed to produce teaching materials for local schools in collaboration with the community:
\begin{itemize}
\item Oxford project page, Enggano in the Austronesian family: \url{https://research.ox.ac.uk/map/enggano-in-the-austronesian-family-historical-and-typological-perspectives}
\item Project paper, Enggano Project in Indonesia: \url{https://enggano.ling-phil.ox.ac.uk/static/papers/Enggano%20Project%20in%20Indonesia.pdf}
\end{itemize}
The actual teaching materials were not located in the search results.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Language Documentation \& Description article on Enggano: \url{https://www.lddjournal.org/article/id/1009/}
\item Paper on the Enggano online dictionary (ACIS 2024): \url{https://aisel.aisnet.org/acis2024/9}
\item Presentation on the Enggano dictionary: \url{https://enggano.ling-phil.ox.ac.uk/static/papers/20241016%20-%20ACIS%20Enggano%20Dictionary.pdf}
\item Endangered Languages Project entry: \url{https://endangeredlanguages.com/node/114524}
\end{itemize}
Earlier works mentioned in the results: Kähler's 1940 sketch grammar and 1987 posthumous dictionary, and a 2021 dictionary from the Bengkulu language office (Riswari et al.); their links were not found. An Enggano-Indonesian-English dictionary by the Oxford team is reported as published.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
None were found in the search.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
An online dictionary is described in the ACIS 2024 paper above; the project also planned an annotated corpus. The Bodleian Taylor Institution blog mentions the project: \url{https://blogs.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/taylorian/2025/04/}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The project introduced a standard orthography and reportedly held an end-of-project celebration on the island in January 2025, with continued support planned. The language is not known to be taught in schools, so the project's school materials and dictionary are the natural base for a curriculum.
