\section{Introduction}
Tanacross is an endangered Athabaskan language of interior Alaska with about 60 speakers. Dictionary and word-list resources exist, including an online dictionary with audio.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No school curriculum or primer was found in search. The learners' dictionary below is the closest to a classroom resource.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Tanacross Learners' Dictionary} (2009, Alaska Native Language Center): about 2000 English headwords, nearly 4500 Tanacross words, example sentences and verb paradigms: \url{https://www.uaf.edu/anlc/research-and-resources/research/research/tanacross_learners_dictionary.php}
\item Online version of the Learners' Dictionary, searchable, with audio for most entries. It is based on the preliminary version of the published dictionary: \url{https://knowledgebase.arts.ubc.ca/?p=8128}
\item Catalogue entry for the Learners' Dictionary: \url{https://knowledgebase.arts.ubc.ca/?p=2832}
\item Tanacross noun dictionary (early word-lists of 1973, 1977 and 1979): \url{https://knowledgebase.arts.ubc.ca/tanacross-noun-dictionary/}
\item Alaska Native Language Center page on Tanacross: \url{https://uaf.edu/anlc/languages-move/tanacross.php}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
None were found in search.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
The online learners' dictionary includes audio recordings (see above).

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Few speakers remain, and no school teaching was found. The learners' dictionary and its audio can serve as a base for materials. Whether the Alaska Native Language Archive holds other Tanacross materials should be checked in its own catalogue.
