\section{Introduction}
Yuyu (ISO 639-3 yxu) is a dormant or extinct Pama-Nyungan language of the Lower Murray region of southern South Australia. Its status is disputed. Capell (1963) treated it as a distinct language. Berndt and Berndt (1993) treated it as a dialect or tribal name. Walsh (1981) treated it as a cover term for four dialects. Search found no modern wordlist and no teaching materials.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Wikipedia, ``Yuyu language'': overview of status and classification. \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuyu_language}
\item Horgen, Michael (2004), \emph{The languages of the Lower Murray}, La Trobe University MA thesis (335 pp.): a Glottolog reference entry. AUSTLANG notes that it contains some grammatical description of Yuyu. \url{https://glottolog.org/resource/reference/id/306364}
\item Moorhouse (1846), \emph{A vocabulary, and outline of the grammatical structure of the Murray River language}, digitised by the National Library of Australia. I did not confirm that its language is the same as Yuyu, so this needs checking. \url{https://nla.gov.au/nla.obj-1126543819/view}
\item AIATSIS AUSTLANG entry S19, which came up in the Yuyu search. I did not verify which language it describes. \url{https://collection.aiatsis.gov.au/austlang/language/s19}
\end{itemize}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Material is sparse and historical, and the identity of Yuyu is contested. A curriculum would have to be built together with Lower Murray Aboriginal communities and regional language programmes, starting from the sources above.
