\section{Introduction}
Gowli (ISO 639-3 gok) is, according to Glottolog, a retired or bookkeeping entry based on a misunderstanding: Glottolog argues that the Gowli do not have a language mutually unintelligible with all surrounding languages. The names Gowli, Gawli and Gavli are used for a pastoral caste (a Yadav subcaste) as well as for the language, and sources do not always distinguish them. No curriculum materials in a distinct Gowli language were found.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
None found.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
Glottolog's bookkeeping page for the code: \url{https://glottolog.org/resource/languoid/iso/gok}. The Rosetta Project/Panglossia entry: \url{https://rosettapanglossia.longnow.org/index.php?title=Gok} (content not examined beyond the search result). No grammar or dictionary was found.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
Scripture Earth lists Gowli scripture (a New Testament) with alternative names such as Nand, Khamla, Lingaayat and Ranya: \url{https://www.scriptureearth.org/00eng.php?iso=gok}. Joshua Project describes the Gawli people, mainly in northern Karnataka, at about 19,000 persons: \url{https://joshuaproject.net/index.php/people_groups/16799}.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
None found beyond the Scripture Earth listing above.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The status of Gowli as a separate language is unsettled, and the community's actual home language(s) (possibly Kannada, Marathi or Hindi varieties) must be established locally before any native-language curriculum is planned. This report is necessarily short because little exists.
