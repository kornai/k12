\section{Introduction}
Algonquin (ISO 639-3 alq) is an Algic language of Quebec and Ontario, Canada. Several communities teach it in schools. This report lists resources that appeared in search results.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Kitigan Zibi Algonquin language and cultural program: core Algonquin from Grade 1 through Secondary 2, with a Language and Culture Coordinator developing materials: \url{https://kitiganzibi.ca/education/algonquin-language-cultural-program/}
\item Kitigan Zibi Kikinamadinan school, including the Mokasige half-day immersion programme for Grades 1--6: \url{https://kitiganzibi.ca/education/kitigan-zibi-kikanamadinan/}
\end{itemize}

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Wikipedia, Algonquin language: \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquin_language}
\item Community-based Algonquin dictionary covering seven dialects, sold by the Algonquin Anishinabeg Nation Tribal Council (described here): \url{https://mediacoop.ca/story/algonquin-language-resources-anishinaabemowin/36702}
\item Study of the Kitigan Zibi immersion programme as a language-survival case: \url{https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/3199633.pdf}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Kitigan Zibi Anishinabeg community (Wikipedia): \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitigan_Zibi_Anishinabeg}
\item Algonquin College Kwey initiative: \url{https://www.algonquincollege.com/tri/initiatives-kwey/}

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
Radio station CKWE 103.9FM in Maniwaki reportedly broadcasts short weekday lessons, and a dictionary audio app was reported as in development; see \url{https://mediacoop.ca/story/algonquin-language-resources-anishinaabemowin/36702}.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Dialect classification is disputed, and the dictionary omits some dialects (Pikwakanagan, Wahgoshig). Any curriculum should be built with community language staff, starting from the Kitigan Zibi programme.
