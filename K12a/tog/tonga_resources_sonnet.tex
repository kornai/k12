\section{Introduction}
This dossier covers Tonga (ISO 639-3 tog; Glottocode tong1321) of Nkhata Bay District, Malawi, called chiTonga by its speakers, with about 170,000 speakers. It must not be confused with Tonga of Zambia and Zimbabwe (toi) or of Mozambique (toh). The search found very little: general encyclopedic descriptions only. No school materials, modern dictionary, orthography or scripture were found.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
None found in search results. No primers, textbooks or school programmes in this language were located.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
Wikipedia describes the language and notes that Turner's 1952 dictionary lists only the words that differ from Tumbuka. The language is often treated as close to, or a dialect of, Tumbuka (Guthrie zone N15). Sources disagree on this. The University of Malawi language mapping survey is said to hold sample vocabulary and a folktale, but this was not confirmed.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
There is no evidence of the language being taught in schools. Any curriculum would have to start from basic documentation: orthography, lexicon and texts, probably in coordination with Tumbuka-language education in Malawi. Fieldwork and community consultation are needed.
