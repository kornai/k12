\section{Introduction}
Engenni (Egene; ISO 639-3 enn; Glottocode enge1239) is a Niger-Congo language of Rivers State, Nigeria. Sources found disagree on speaker numbers (Omniglot about 211,000; Joshua Project about 59,000 people) and on dialect divisions. The search found very little material for schools.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
Literacy titles from the 1970s Rivers Readers Project (\emph{How to Read Engenni}, \emph{How to Write Engenni}, 1978) are mentioned in a summary of the search results, but no copy or catalogue page was found. No current textbooks were found.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
A New Testament, \emph{Baibulu Eba Fai}, was reported as published in 1977 (see the Wikipedia and Omniglot pages). Community and demographic background:
\begin{itemize}
\item Wikipedia, Engenni people: \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engenni_people}
\item Joshua Project, Engenni in Nigeria: \url{https://joshuaproject.net/people_groups/11762/NI}
\end{itemize}

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
No audio, video or software resources were found.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The language is reportedly vigorous, but no school use was found. A curriculum would start from the orthography (adopted alphabet reportedly revised in 2011), the Bible translation and the 1970s readers, and would require new materials to be written with community partners.
