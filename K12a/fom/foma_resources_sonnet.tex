\section{Introduction}
Foma (ISO 639-3 \texttt{fom}; also called Lifoma) is reported to be spoken along the Congo River near Basoko in Tshopo Province, Democratic Republic of the Congo. The search found very little published material on the language. This report lists only what was found and says plainly where nothing was found.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No primers, textbooks or school materials in Foma were found in search results.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
No grammar, orthography description or dedicated dictionary was found. Glosbe has automatically generated language-pair pages for Foma, but the search snippets did not show actual dictionary content, so their quality is unverified: \url{https://glosbe.com/fom/pha}.

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
Joshua Project has a profile of the Foma people in the DRC and states that no Scripture in Foma is documented; Lingala and French are used for church life in the region: \url{https://joshuaproject.net/people_groups/11221}.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
No audio, video or corpus resources were found.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
There is no evidence in these results of Foma being taught in schools or of an established orthography. Any curriculum would first need fieldwork-based documentation, orthography development and community consultation. Related Bantu documentation could be investigated, but nothing specific was found here.
