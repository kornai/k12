\section{Introduction}
Wuvulu-Aua (ISO 639-3 wuv, Glottocode wuvu1239) is an Oceanic Austronesian language of Manus Province, Papua New Guinea. Wikipedia gives about 1,600 speakers. The Onne and Auna dialects are spoken on Wuvulu Island and the Aua dialect on Aua Island. See \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuvulu-Aua_language}.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No primers, textbooks or school materials were found in these searches. The language is not known to be taught in schools.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
James A. Hafford, \emph{Wuvulu Grammar and Vocabulary} (PhD dissertation, University of Hawai\textquoteleft i at M\=anoa, 2014, 242 pp.), is the main description found and includes a vocabulary: \url{https://search.worldcat.org/title/Wuvulu-Grammar-and-Vocabulary/oclc/1257963345}, with a Glottolog record at \url{https://glottolog.org/resource/reference/id/475990}. His earlier MA thesis, \emph{Elements of Wuvulu Grammar} (University of Texas at Arlington, 1999), is cited at \url{https://grambank.clld.org/sources/gHaffordWuvulu} and \url{https://tlopo.clld.org/sources/Hafford1999}. Grambank's language page is \url{https://grambank.clld.org/languages/wuvu1239}. No separate dictionary was found. Whether the 2014 work covers Aua as well as Wuvulu was not confirmed.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Documentation is academic only. The Hafford grammar and vocabulary could underpin an orthography and word list. A curriculum would need community partnership and attention to the dialect differences.
