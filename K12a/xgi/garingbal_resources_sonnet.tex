\section{Introduction}
Garingbal (Garaynbal, Karingbal; ISO 639-3 xgi) is an extinct Aboriginal language of central Queensland, Australia. It is described as a dialect of Biri. Its country lay around the headwaters of the Comet River and the upper Mackenzie River. No native speakers remain, and I found no school curriculum for it. This report lists only what appeared in search results, and little was found.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
I found no primers, textbooks or school programmes for Garingbal. The State Library of Queensland featured the word \emph{nhani} ('land, earth, country') in a 2019 Word Week post. That is a single-word teaching item, not a curriculum: \url{https://www.slq.qld.gov.au/blog/2019-international-year-indigenous-languages-word-week-week-forty-eight}

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
Wikipedia summarises the people and language: \url{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garaynbal}. The State Library of Queensland says most of the language content comes from work by Tindale, Breen and Terrill. The AIATSIS thesaurus entry, hosted by the Aboriginal \& Torres Strait Islander Data Archive, lists Garingbal under the broader term Biri / Birri Gubba: \url{https://www.atsida.edu.au/node/175}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
I found no scripture translations or community organisation pages specific to Garingbal.

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
I found no audio, video or software resources for Garingbal.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Documentation is thin and comes mainly from linguists' records. A heritage curriculum would have to be built with the descendant community and with archives. Related Biri-chain materials may be useful, but they should not be presented as Garingbal. Only the sources above were seen.
