\section{Introduction}
Carian (ISO 639-3 xcr; Glottocode cari1274) is an extinct Anatolian language of southwestern Anatolia, written in its own alphabet. It was deciphered mainly through Egyptian--Carian bilingual tomb inscriptions, beginning with John Ray in 1981. Adiego and Schürr then developed the Ray--Schürr--Adiego values, which the Kaunos Carian--Greek bilingual of 1996 largely confirmed. Much of the evidence is onomastic, and many letter values are still provisional. I found no school materials.

\end{itemize}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The language is extinct, the corpus is small and mostly names, and the decipherment is partial. A K-12 unit could use the script, bilingual inscriptions and the decipherment story, with the sources above as the basis. It could not teach the language itself.
