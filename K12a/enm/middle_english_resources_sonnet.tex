\section{Introduction}
Middle English (ISO 639-3 enm, Glottocode midd1317) was spoken roughly 1100--1500 and has no native speakers. A heritage and revitalization curriculum would therefore be reading-oriented. This search found only a few resources, all centred on the University of Michigan Middle English Compendium.
\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No school textbooks or primers were found in the search results. The Compendium has been used by students for class assignments (see the University of Michigan news item):
\url{https://news.umich.edu/?p=3125}
\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Middle English Compendium (Middle English Dictionary, bibliography, and searchable corpus of prose and verse, 1100--1500), freely accessible: \url{https://quod.lib.umich.edu/m/med}
\item About the Middle English Compendium: \url{https://quod.lib.umich.edu/m/middle-english-dictionary/about}
\end{itemize}
\section{Religious and Community Resources}
None were found in this search.
\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
The Compendium is the main digital resource. No audio resources were found in this search.
\section{Curriculum Development Context}
The dictionary and corpus can support text-reading units. Teaching materials for school level would need to be developed or located by further searching.
