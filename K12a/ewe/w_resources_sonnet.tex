\section{Introduction}
Ewe (ISO 639-3 \texttt{ewe}, Glottocode ewee1241) is spoken in Ghana and Togo and is taught in Ghanaian primary and secondary schools; in Togo its integration into education is reported as incomplete. Curricular adoption of the items below was not verified.
\section{Primary Educational Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Ewe Basic Course} (Warburton, Kpotufe, Glover, 1968), with audio, hosted by the Indiana University CELTIE site. \url{https://clcl.uiowa.edu/node/471}
\item \emph{Basic Ewe for Foreign Students} (revised Claudi and Ossey, 2015), K\"oppe. \url{https://www.koeppe.de/titel_basic-ewe-for-foreign-students-2}
\end{itemize}
\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Duthie, \emph{Introducing Ewe Linguistic Patterns} (Ghana Universities Press, 1996), for native speakers and learners. \url{https://www.africanbookscollective.com/books/introducing-ewe-linguistic-patterns}
\item Lexilogos Ewe dictionary links (including Westermann's 1905 Ewe-German dictionary). \url{https://www.lexilogos.com/english/ewe_dictionary.htm}
\item Global Voices interview on a new French-Ewe dictionary (2024, Togo). \url{https://lingua.globalvoices.org/2025/01/interview-with-the-author-of-a-new-french-ewe-dictionary-in-togo/}
\item Omniglot, Ewe. \url{https://www.omniglot.com:443/writing/ewe.htm}
\end{itemize}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Ewe has an established orthography and a body of grammars and dictionaries, making it comparatively well-resourced; locating the official Ghana and Togo school syllabi is the next step.
