\section{Introduction}
Yanomamö (ISO 639-3 guu) belongs to the Yanomaman family and is spoken in Venezuela and Brazil. Search results indicate that the Yanomami Territory in Brazil has several related languages and many dialect variants (Sanöma, Ninam, Yanomam and others), so materials must be matched to the right variety. This report is based on a small number of web search results; much of what was found is academic, and no ready-made K-12 textbook series was located.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Academic article on a collaboration between linguists, educators and anthropologists to produce a first-stage literacy primer in Sanöma (a Yanomami language of Brazil): \url{https://repositorio.ufmg.br/handle/1843/62336?mode=full}
\item Study of a syllabic literacy method adapted to the Parima B dialect in Venezuela (reported as used by the New Tribes Mission in the 1990s): \url{https://saber.ucv.ve/jspui/handle/10872/18057}
\item Master's thesis on literacy practices among the Yanomami: \url{https://repositorio.ufpe.br/jspui/bitstream/123456789/15294/1/Disserta%c3%a7%c3%a3o_Pr%c3%a1ticas_de_Letramentos_entre_os_Yanomae%202%20%281%29.pdf}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
No religious texts or community-organisation pages were verified in the search results. Hutukara is mentioned in the sources above as a partner in language work, but no Hutukara page was found.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Sources describe the language as first language in communities such as Maturacá, with Portuguese as L2. Materials exist mainly as academic descriptions of primers, dictionaries and literacy work; the actual primers and dictionaries should be located through the institutions named above and checked for variety (Sanöma, Ninam, Yanomam, Parima B) before use.
