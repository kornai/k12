\section{Introduction}
Toaripi (also called East Elema; ISO 639-3 tqo) is an Eleman language spoken in the Gulf Province of Papua New Guinea. The search found Bible translation material and an academic paper, but no dedicated dictionary, primer or school curriculum.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No Toaripi primers, textbooks or school programmes were found in the search results.

\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item H.A. Brown, a 1977 Pacific Linguistics paper on the Eleman language family, with a section titled ``The Beginning of Literacy''. I could not confirm what it says about primers: \url{https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/a028f429-a324-40e8-a7bd-96ff5690977f/content}
\item Overview of the language on Wikipedia: \url{https://wikipedia.com/wiki/ISO_639:tqo}
\end{itemize}

\section{Religious and Community Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Toaripi Bible (\emph{Safu Buka}, 1983) on the YouVersion platform. It includes a glossary of difficult words, \emph{O Hea Ve Eite}: \url{https://www.bible.com/versions/3653}
\item Joshua Project entry for Toaripi: \url{https://joshuaproject.net/people_groups/15516}
\item Joshua Project language page for tqo: \url{https://www.joshuaproject.net/index.php/languages/tqo}
\item A news report on the digitised Toaripi New Testament: \url{https://www.mnnonline.org/?p=78895}
\end{itemize}

\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
The Bible is available in digital form (YouVersion). No Toaripi audio recordings were found.

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
With about 64,000 speakers, Toaripi has a sizeable community. Written material is largely the Bible and its glossary, so a school curriculum would need new work, built on this written base and on community knowledge. No existing school resources were found.
