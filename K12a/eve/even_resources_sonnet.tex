\section{Introduction}
Even (ISO 639-3 \texttt{eve}, Glottocode even1260) is an endangered Tungusic language of the Russian Federation with roughly 10,000 speakers. Web searches found mainly historical and scholarly material about Even educational publishing; no current Even-language K-12 textbook set was found in search results. This dossier is therefore short.
\section{Primary Educational Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Article on the history of publication of educational materials in Even (Cyberleninka). Covers the 1858 Tungus primer (described in sources as Even, Ola dialect), Soviet-era primers and readers, and notes that Even-medium schooling has been limited to primary level. \url{https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/from-the-history-of-publication-of-educational-materials-on-even-language}
\item Paper by Tarasova (dh-north.org) discussing early Even primer history. \url{https://dh-north.org/siberian_studies/publications/betarasova.pdf}
\item SPRI library catalogue record for a Leningrad-published Even (Lamut) school reader (N.P. Tkachik, 1948). \url{https://www.spri.cam.ac.uk/library/catalogue/records/21450/}
\end{itemize}

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
\begin{itemize}
\item Overview of the sociolinguistic situation of Tungus-Manchu languages in Russia, including school teaching of Even. \url{https://www.altaist.org/home/confessions/girfanova-albina-h/tungus-manchu-languages-in-russia-sociolinguistical-situation/}
\end{itemize}
Available sources indicate Even-medium instruction exists at most at primary level, and secondary-level materials are lacking. Any curriculum would need to build new materials.
