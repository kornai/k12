\section{Introduction}
American Sign Language (ISO 639-3 ase) is the language of Deaf communities in the United States. This compilation is based on a single search and is a sample, not a comprehensive survey.
\section{Primary Educational Resources}
\begin{itemize}
\item Gallaudet K-12 ASL standards and curriculum framework (as described in a state list): \url{https://mdelio.org/node/861}
\item Gallaudet ASL \& Deaf Studies K-12 Curriculum Framework: \url{https://caslsintercom.uoregon.edu/content/8451}
\item Lifeprint ASL University (free lessons): \url{https://amser.org/r1004/asl_university}
\item American Sign Language at Home, a free curriculum for ages 0--3: \url{https://aslaccess.org/links}
\end{itemize}
\section{Linguistic and Reference Materials}
\begin{itemize}
\item Lifeprint ASL dictionary: \url{https://Lifeprint.com/dictionary.htm}
\item ASL resources list (University of Florida): \url{https://deafblind.ufl.edu/topics/american-sign-language-asl-resources/}
\end{itemize}
\section{Religious and Community Resources}
None were found in this search.
\section{Digital and Audio Resources}
The Lifeprint dictionary and ASL University are online. Commercial video dictionaries (e.g.\ Signing Savvy) are mentioned on the mdelio.org list above.
\section{Curriculum Development Context}
Standards and frameworks exist for ASL as a first and second language. Some listed products are commercial and not endorsed by state boards, so materials should be checked against local approval lists.
