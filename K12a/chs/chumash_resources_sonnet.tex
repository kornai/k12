\section{Introduction}
Chumashan languages (ISO 639-3 chs) have no first-language speakers today; tribal members and activists pursue revitalization. Barbare\~no and Inese\~no (Samala) are the best documented varieties, largely through J.\ P.\ Harrington's unpublished fieldnotes. This report is short: only encyclopedic and reference pages were found in search, and no school curriculum was located.

\section{Primary Educational Resources}
No primers, textbooks or school curricula were found in the search results. Programmes mentioned by the sources (to be verified directly): the Santa Ynez Band of Chumash Indians Language Program (Samala), the Barbare\~no Chumash Council, and the \v{S}muwi\v{c} Language School at Wishtoyo Chumash Village in Malibu (opened 2010 according to the sources).

\section{Curriculum Development Context}
A K-12 curriculum would need to be built in partnership with the tribal language programmes, from archival (Harrington) sources. The content available through search is limited.
